## Welcome

LIS 815 Indexing and Abstracting introduces you to the ways information professionals describe
documents so that people can find them again. You will learn what subject indexing is and why it
matters, how controlled vocabularies and thesauri are built, how pre-coordinate and post-coordinate
systems organise terms, how retrieval performance is measured, and how abstracts are written —
including how digital tools and AI are changing the work.

The course is written for postgraduate students who are new to the subject, and plain language is
used throughout while keeping the technical vocabulary you need.

## How the course is structured

The material is organised into seven modules containing fourteen chapters:

1. **Module 1 — Theoretical Foundations and Types of Indexes**
2. **Module 2 — Vocabulary Control**
3. **Module 3 — Pre-Coordinate Indexing Systems**
4. **Module 4 — Post-Coordinate and Derived Indexing**
5. **Module 5 — Performance Evaluation and System Metrics**
6. **Module 6 — Abstracting Principles and Applications**
7. **Module 7 — Digital, Automated and AI-Assisted Indexing**

Each chapter gives you learning objectives, explanations, worked examples and review questions so
you can check your understanding as you go.

## How you are assessed

Assessment covers practical indexing work, an abstracting portfolio, a mid-term task and a final
examination. The exact weights, preparation areas and examination rules are set out in the
**Assessment guide** page — read it early so you know what you are working towards.

## What is not examinable

Some pages and materials in this LMS are supplementary enrichment: extra background, optional
reading and enrichment activities. They are always labelled as supplementary, and they are never
examinable. Anything you need for assessment is drawn from the core chapters and the assessment
materials described in the assessment guide.
