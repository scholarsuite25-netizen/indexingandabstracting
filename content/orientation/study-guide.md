## Read the chapters in order

The fourteen chapters run in a deliberate sequence: the foundations in Module 1 lead into vocabulary
control, then pre-coordinate systems, then post-coordinate and derived indexing, then evaluation,
then abstracting, then digital and AI-assisted indexing. Each chapter builds on the ones before it,
so work through them from Chapter 1 onwards rather than jumping around.

## Work through the review questions

Every chapter ends with review questions. Treat them as part of your study, not an afterthought:
attempt them while the chapter is still fresh, then go back to the relevant section and re-read
anything you could not answer. Answering in your own words is good practice — the course rewards
understanding over memorised wording.

## Use knowledge checks formatively

Knowledge checks sit at the end of each chapter. Use them to find the gaps in your understanding
rather than to chase a score: read the explanation for every answer, including the ones you got
right, and revisit the chapter section when you slip.

## Keep a glossary of terms

Indexing has a dense technical vocabulary — exhaustivity, specificity, pre- and post-coordinate,
derived indexing, BT/NT/RT/USE/UF, and many more. Keep your own running glossary as you read, and
check it against the course glossary when you finish a module.

## Practise the calculation questions

Retrieval measures such as precision, recall and fallout are calculation skills, and calculation
skills only settle in with practice. Work the numerical examples yourself first, then check the
worked example, and repeat until the steps are routine.

## PRECIS operator rules

The preface is explicit that, for systems such as PRECIS, you should follow the exact operator
rules prescribed by your lecturer or course manual, because notational conventions vary between
institutions. Study the principles and the practical sequence of a PRECIS string in the chapters,
but treat your own lecturer's or manual's operator notation as the standard you are working to.
