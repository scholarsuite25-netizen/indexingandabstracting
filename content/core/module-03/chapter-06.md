## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to:
- Define pre-coordinate indexing.
- Explain chain indexing.
- Explain cyclic (cycled) indexing.
- Discuss the advantages and limitations of pre-coordinate systems.

## 6.1 What Is Pre-Coordinate Indexing?

In pre-coordinate indexing, the concepts that make up a subject are combined into a single indexing expression at the moment the document is indexed — that is, before any user ever performs a search. The indexer, not the searcher, is responsible for deciding how the different concepts relate to one another within that expression.

## EXAMPLE

University Libraries — Automation — Nigeria. The relationship between these three concepts, and the order in which they appear, is determined entirely by the indexer at the time of indexing.

## 6.2 Key Characteristics

Pre-coordinate systems can produce carefully controlled and well-structured representations of a document’s subject, and are common in traditional subject-heading systems. Their main limitation is that the indexer must try to anticipate which combinations of concepts will actually be useful to future users, which is not always possible to predict with certainty, and skilled, trained indexers are needed to apply the rules correctly.

## 6.3 Chain Indexing

Chain indexing is closely associated with the Indian librarian and classification theorist S. R. Ranganathan. This technique derives subject entries from the hierarchical chain of concepts found within a classification scheme, and it creates access points directly from that conceptual structure, illustrating the close relationship between classification and subject indexing.

## 6.4 Cyclic (Cycled) Indexing

Cyclic indexing approaches generate multiple index entries by varying the position or focus of terms within a compound subject, so that each significant component is, in turn, brought into the leading position. This provides several different access points that all lead back to the same document, increasing the chance that a user will find it regardless of which term they search first.

## WORKED EXAMPLE

The subject “Use of Artificial Intelligence in University Libraries in Nigeria” could generate cycled access points such as: Artificial Intelligence — University Libraries — Nigeria; University Libraries — Artificial Intelligence — Nigeria; and Nigeria — University Libraries — Artificial Intelligence.

## 6.5 Advantages and Limitations

Pre-coordinate systems offer several advantages, alongside some real limitations:
- Advantage — controlled and predictable representation of subjects.
- Advantage — explicit relationships between concepts are preserved.
- Advantage — access points are consistent and predictable for users.
- Advantage — can provide highly precise subject access.
- Limitation — considerable intellectual effort is required from the indexer.
- Limitation — the system depends heavily on the rules being applied correctly.
- Limitation — the combinations a user actually searches for may differ from those the indexer anticipated.
- Limitation — users may need to understand the indexing structure to search it effectively.
