## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to:
- Explain the purpose of a scope note.
- Use USE and UF references correctly.
- Distinguish between alphabetical and classified thesaurus displays.
- Describe the basic tasks involved in thesaurus maintenance.

## 5.1 Scope Notes

A scope note is a short explanatory statement attached to a term to clarify exactly how that term is to be interpreted or applied within a particular vocabulary. Scope notes are especially useful when a term is ambiguous in everyday language or when it carries a specialised meaning within the subject field.

## 5.2 USE and UF References

USE directs a searcher or indexer from a non-preferred term toward the preferred term that should be used instead. UF, which stands for Used For, works in the opposite direction: it is displayed underneath the preferred term to show which non-preferred terms lead to it.

## EXAMPLE

AUTOMOBILES — UF: Cars. | CARS — USE: Automobiles.

## 5.3 Alphabetical Display

In an alphabetical display, terms are simply arranged from A to Z. This arrangement is convenient for a user who already knows the specific term they are interested in and wants to look up its relationships quickly.

## 5.4 Classified Display

In a classified display, terms are grouped together within a broader conceptual structure rather than alphabetically. This arrangement supports browsing, allowing a user to explore related areas of a subject even when they do not yet know the exact term they need.

## 5.5 Maintaining a Thesaurus

A thesaurus is never a finished product. Because terminology changes over time, thesauri require continuing revision. Typical maintenance tasks include adding new terms as they emerge, revising definitions and scope notes, merging or splitting concepts as understanding of a field develops, updating cross-references, and keeping a documented record of why each decision was made.

## 5.6 The Role of Software

Thesaurus-management software can support many of the mechanical aspects of this work, including data entry, checking that relationships are consistent, version control, and publication of the finished thesaurus. However, such software supports the intellectual work of the indexer; it does not replace the human judgement required to decide what a term means and how it relates to other terms.

## Review Questions

1. Write a scope note for a term that could easily be misunderstood.
2. Create your own USE and UF pair.
3. Give one advantage of an alphabetical display and one advantage of a classified display.
