## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to:
- Explain the meaning of semantics and syntax as they apply to indexing.
- Identify equivalence, hierarchical, and associative relationships between terms.
- Explain syntactic control, role operators, and citation order.

## 3.1 Semantics: The Study of Meaning

In everyday language, semantics is simply the study of meaning. In indexing, semantic relationships describe how concepts and the terms that represent them relate to one another. Three relationships are especially important in indexing work: equivalence, hierarchy, and association.

## 3.2 Equivalence

Equivalence connects two or more different terms that can represent the same concept, or concepts that are close enough to be treated as the same for retrieval purposes. In a controlled vocabulary, one of these terms is usually chosen as the preferred term, while the others are marked as non-preferred and simply point the user toward the preferred form.

## 3.3 Hierarchy

A hierarchical relationship connects a general concept to a more specific one. A Broader Term (often abbreviated BT) represents the more general concept, while a Narrower Term (NT) represents the more specific one.

## EXAMPLE

Libraries is a Broader Term for University Libraries; University Libraries is a Narrower Term for Libraries.

## 3.4 Association

A Related Term (RT) connects two concepts that are meaningfully associated with one another but are neither synonyms nor in a broader–narrower relationship. For instance, the concepts Libraries and Librarians are closely related in meaning, even though neither is a broader or narrower version of the other.

## 3.5 Syntax: The Study of Order and Combination

While semantics is about meaning, syntax is about how concepts are combined or ordered when they are expressed together. Good syntactic control prevents an index entry from becoming a meaningless list of separate words, and instead preserves the relationships that exist among the concepts being described.

## 3.6 Role Operators

Role operators identify the function that each concept plays within a subject statement — for example, distinguishing an action from the person or thing performing it, or from the object being acted upon. Role operators are especially important in structured indexing systems such as PRECIS. Because different courses and institutions may use slightly different notations, students should always learn and apply the exact operator table prescribed in their own course manual.

## 3.7 Citation Order

Citation order refers to the sequence in which concepts appear within a coordinated indexing expression. Changing that order can change the emphasis of the statement, and in some cases can change its meaning altogether, which is why citation order is treated as a deliberate design choice rather than an arbitrary one.
