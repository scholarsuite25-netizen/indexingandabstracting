## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to:
- Explain how a search strategy is formulated.
- Use basic Boolean operators.
- Explain what derived indexing is, including its advantages and disadvantages.
- Describe KWIC and KWOC.

## 9.1 What Is a Search Strategy?

A search strategy is a planned approach to translating a real information need into searchable concepts, specific terms, and useful combinations of those terms. Searching is generally an iterative activity, meaning that a searcher typically refines their approach over several attempts rather than getting the perfect result in a single step.

## 9.2 Boolean Operators

Three basic Boolean operators are used to combine search terms:
- AND narrows a search by requiring that both concepts be present.
- OR broadens a search by accepting either concept.
- NOT excludes a specified concept from the results.

## EXAMPLE

LIBRARIES AND ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE narrows a search to documents containing both concepts. CARS OR AUTOMOBILES broadens a search to accept either term.

## 9.3 Formulating a Search

A typical search formulation follows these steps:
1. Start by identifying the major concepts in the information need.
2. Identify synonyms and related terms for each concept.
3. Run an initial search using these terms.
4. Inspect the records retrieved for useful results and new vocabulary.
5. Refine the query, using controlled vocabulary terms where they are available.

## 9.4 Derived Indexing

Derived indexing obtains its index terms directly from features of the document itself — most commonly the title, but also abstracts, full text, author-supplied keywords, and captions. This approach can be fast and economical, since it requires little additional intellectual effort and is well suited to automation, but its quality depends entirely on how well the source field actually represents the document’s subject. Because derived terms are taken from the author’s own words rather than a controlled vocabulary, they may include irrelevant words, fail to capture implied context, or run into synonym and ambiguity problems that a trained indexer would normally resolve.

## 9.5 KWIC (Keyword in Context)

KWIC stands for Keyword in Context. In a KWIC display, a significant keyword is shown together with the words surrounding it in the title, so that the keyword retains the context in which it originally appeared.

## 9.6 KWOC (Keyword Out of Context)

KWOC stands for Keyword Out of Context. In a KWOC display, the keyword is shown separately from the rest of the title, typically as a heading, followed underneath by the full citation.

## 9.7 Comparing KWIC and KWOC

KWIC keeps each keyword embedded within its surrounding title, preserving context at a glance. KWOC instead places the keyword outside the title as a separate heading. Both techniques create multiple access points from terms that have been derived directly from the document, rather than assigned by an indexer.
